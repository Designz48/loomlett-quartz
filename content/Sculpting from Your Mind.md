# How reality takes form 
Holding my hand up in front of me, pencil in other, I sketched along the outline and smudged in shadows. I had experimented with chibi art, didn't work. Anime, not great. Now, I was curious in sketching real life, and the drawing turned out exactly as my hand looked.

Having one's arm up is easy to draw since the image doesn't have to be imagined, and while it can be hard for those to sketch even shapes they see in front of their eyes- how much more so when it's all from imagination, right?

Yet, I succeeded in that feat too, because of the skill I learned in putting the image in my mind on paper.

---
**Molding my thumb around the interior**, the clay's soft, damp body didn't resist. The technique was basic and thoroughly taught in the "pottery capital of the nation." Having been around 11 years old, I was jealous of the other kids perfectly smooth shapes whereas mine looked hideous. However, I didn't realize that I had something that they lacked: creativity, which actually *enabled* me to fool around with my clay. The experience of pottery stuck with me for years after, especially when I started using [Blender](https://www.blender.org/features/) and quickly found sculpting easy to gravitate too. After all, there's literally a **clay** tool!

Even after I became passionate with #blender though, sculpting was hard to just ***jump into***. Even though I successfully modeled my first character all from my mind's eye onto the screen, any more tries would result in frustration from either not enough pixels, not understanding tools, or my own procrastination. So I went a different route, [[hard-surface modeling]], a skill hard to implement yet hard to mess up. Most objects can be modeled this way, yet they don't have to be rigid, because of Modifiers like Subdivide, the number of vertices are multiplied and enables the shape to look more organic, or free-forming.

Even after my excursion into [[hard-surface modeling]], I still yearned for sculpting characters, I've observed people in real life and now wanted to study them. So, I bought a course. Yep good 'ol courses, many would say "just watch YT videos" but these never did much good for me, and I had come across a really good one that explained how human's parts (bone, muscle, tissue) can be sculpted one-by-one to add up to a realistic model. Not only that, but the doubts I had in technique I realized didn't matter because it wasn't that I wasn't good at the techniques, but that I didn't have the patience to see them through.

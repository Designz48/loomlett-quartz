## Are they lil’ monsters or angels?

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*_rKqJFwAGCkNIm0IKGpIaw.png)

> Like imaginary lines mentally restricting how you walk, these lil’ guys can do a lot of damage, right?

By keeping you from your dreams, goals, and even drawing you away from certain people, boundaries are often a bad thing.

But they don’t have to be, it’s just in the context you look at them.

So how do we determine what are good & bad boundaries to set?

## Use common sense

Boundaries are walls that keep you from passing, and walls are meant to keep you safe.

If you ever feel uncomfortable about a situation you’re in, up goes the walls! Helping you to make the right decisions.

**But what if you’re in the situation of a lifetime —** a trip to the most safest place in Europe, your internet friends are meeting up for the first time ever, or you simply want to try a new cappuccino at a cafe ☕

Depending on how comfortable you are doing these things, your walls may be low to the ground or as high as a skyscraper!

So now that we’ve applied a little common sense, how do you keep those walls from holding you back to experience more?

## Extend your knowledge

Often than not, the reason you’re uncomfortable getting into a good situation is because you have doubts, which are due to not having enough experience & information.

What if you just watched a true crime documentary about Europe, suddenly you think that all of Europe — even the most proven safest place, is like that too!

Try taking these steps:

> **1.** Read up about the place you’re going and dive deep. Study enough from different reputable sources until you get the big-picture.
> 
> **2.** Ask someone who’s been there. Even if there’s no one IRL you can look on a web forum for someone else’s experience!
> 
> **3.** Look at it from a bird’s eye view, how? Google Maps! The images there are updated & can give you an idea of where you’ll be.

Having knowledge about a place can be just as good as experience. You don’t need to leap into Europe, you just have to take a small step toward it.

## Push outside your comfort zone

Now that we’ve dissected why you’re uncomfortable, whether or not this is a good situation, and then becoming knowledgeable to help you at ease…

**Now’s the time to do it.**

> Again though, don’t leap into it!

_- Ask a group of friends to join you.  
- Prep with the needed supplies (if any)._

If you still feel uncomfortable, know that when you start now, **any opportunities in the future will get easier!**

==It’s often that step over the line that’s the most frightening part==. That is, until you do it again & again to become familiar with it.

## Experimenting with AI’s ability to prevent writing block

> Ever had a story you found when dusting your hard drive to realize you wrote it 5 years ago and never finished it?

That’s how I felt yesterday, wondering _how can I get this into the world for my future self-reflection if I’m no longer in the mindset to finish it?_

When thinking about all I had to do and how much time it would take to get back into that this-is-the-purpose-I’m-writing-this-for mindset (nearly impossible after so long), I asked [Lumo — Proton’s AI bot](https://lumo.proton.me/) to read what I had and then both edit and add onto it. To give a good analysis, I’m going to show you my first draft, here you go!

The vast, light-grey sky was nearing sunset; 8:30 PM. I started to sleep, dozing off on my fluffy comfort of a bed. The walls in my room darkened, yet, the thickly clouded sky was bright as it’s unusual shine glowed on the wall to my right.

The inspiration for this was trying to turn around a gloomy clouded day into one that was full of mystery and adventure. What I missed to detail in this that reflects that, as I realize now reading this over, is the details of outside the window. It’d probably go something like this,

“Light grey pillows surrounded outside the window, seemingly distant but also close. Looking down, I’m not sure whether I could step onto the clouds below or fall through them — it was as if I were either high in the clouds or the ground didn’t exist at all.”

**_Now, what was AI’s reaction to this?_**

“That’s a lot of atmosphere packed into this story…. tapping into childhood feeling of being in an adventure with unease. Unsettling ambiguous sky, surrendering to the unknown. Losing control yet normal, the rules don’t apply. Stepping into something not grounded into reality, metaphor for taking a leap of faith or a lesson in friendship. Less about sadness and more about possibility.”

As much as I believe that AI is mostly full of nonsense… I gotta admit that its last comment, “Less about sadness and more about possibility” was a great way to describe this.

Now here’s Lumo’s edited/adding onto the story.

My first thought when reading over the paragraph was that it changed the time to sunset rather than before sunset. I’m kind of fine with this because it enhances the overall mystery, but I think having the setting be _before_ sunset gives it a lighter, brighter appearance that I seemed to be initially going for. Also, I’m confused by its saying “metallic” as a description for the outside, even though clouds are soft and actually give the feeling of lightness rather than a hard, metallic surface.

Even with Lumo seeming to piece things together that I hadn’t done initially, it missed parts like how they got shoes on, how they got back when the lightning had already struck, and even having some grammar mistakes.

Overall, it seems like Lumo took my details and detailed them _even further_, thus giving more atmosphere to my story than I had originally written. I think this is a great way cover all details and have your audience better envision the setting. Then again, with a few re-reads and editing on the author’s part, this could be done — just not as quick as an AI of course. Also, because Lumo changed specific elements like the feel of the clouds and from warm air to cool, this changes the story quite a bit, even if they seem to be minor details… like how it added it was going to rain, even though my initial story didn’t express that whatsoever.

This was my shortest story, I have longer ones from years ago but I’d want to edit and add details myself. I’m curious how other authors might be using AI for their work, if they do so at all.

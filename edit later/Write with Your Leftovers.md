### Write with Your Leftovers

Having leftover food is easy, right? You think that you made or ordered just enough for dinner, _but sure enough-_ you have that small scrap of food you just can’t take to your mouth.

What happens with these scraps? Well, they’re small, tasty, yet a hassle since they needed to be put in the smallest container you have. So often these leftovers just get thrown out at the end of each meal. What if you did decide to stuff your fridge with a bunch of little containers though?

Turns out, if you save up little by little of these scraps, you might just end up with a big meal at the end of the week.

### What does this have to do with writing?

Like a meal and its leftovers, we always have thoughts swirling around in our head- even if we make the effort to write about them.

Not only this, but as writing enters in all parts of our life: whether at work, designing a home project, or simply writing out a To-Do; if we ignore these extra thoughts and let them get thrown away, we might’ve also thrown away a chance at writing, or doing, something new.

#### Don’t just write the next big idea

They’re lots of influencers, bloggers, etc. looking at fashion trends and the such to get ideas. Think, if everyone went to the same source to find inspiration, their writing ideas (or even product ideas) would be all the same. For example, how many models and Instagram influencers have their own line of makeup? It’s the “next big idea” meaning that it’s a fashion trend that makes a lot of money, but what if they went for inspiration otherwise?

We already have a lot going on to talk about. Even if it’s just as simple as taking a walk and spotting a Bluejay. Has your fellow niched writer (AKA in the same niche as you) went on a walk and had your experience? I doubt it, so that experience is personal to you.

#### Take what you know

Experience comes from doing, and as Jason Fried says in his book _Rework_.

“We wrote that book [Getting Real] without even knowing it. The experience that came from building a company [37Signals] and building software was the waste from actually doing the work.”

Same as ideas can be reused from small sources, they can be reused on a big-picture scale as well. As Jason Fried mentions in his book, Ford (the automotive company) had leftover wood in their productions. So what did they do? Make charcoal! And that’s when Kingsford Charcoal was born.

If you’re writing a book, you know that time when you edit to delete half of it? Well, are their any fresh ideas in that half that you can use for another book?

#### Recycle and reuse

“Software companies usually don’t usually think about writing books. Bands don’t usually think about filming the recording process. Car manufacturers don’t usually think about selling charcoal. There’s probably something you haven’t thought about that you could sell too.”

### Takeaway

There are always ideas and there are always places to reuse ideas. Once you trim down the ones that work and do something with them, you might yourself with a lot of leftovers to do even more with.

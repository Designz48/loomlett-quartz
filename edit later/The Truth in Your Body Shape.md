#### The math ain’t mathing

When studying human shapes for my 3D modeling, I got curious and studied on the differences between different shapes. Unlike as a kid and just thinking about physical appearances, I realized how much our metabolism affects it, and is broken down into 3 different types: ectomorph, mesomorph, and endomorph.

These types are influenced by whether a person has a fast metabolism (ectomorph), slow metabolism (endomorph), or a buff in-between (mesomorph). Then the body shapes, which both obviously and not obviously take after the metabolism _(you might see why in a bit)_ are as follows: rectangle _(straight, no waist)_, triangle/pear _(large hips)_, inverted triangle _(large shoulders)_, apple _(little waist definition)_, and as far as I know as last, the envied hourglass _(perfectly symmetrical with the so-called smallest waist)_.

The first thing one does to figure out which one they are, isn’t looking at scientific articles about it, right? _Not when one doesn’t have time of course…_ so instead I took quizzes. However, over the years of changing interests, whenever I took a quiz I kept getting varying results that didn’t make sense!

For the majority of the quiz results it said I was a rectangle. I would be content with that, except that I didn’t _feel_ like a rectangle. Now, since a rectangle doesn’t have big hips you might be thinking that I wanted that, but _nope!_ I’ve always liked that my hips were smaller, but I also noticed that my shoulders were wide, wider than a usual gal anyhow. Enough of a difference to know that I was not a rectangle.

Recently this year, after years of thinking I was a rectangle, I did another quiz, and it said I was a pear, _another_ said a rectangle, and then another an… apple? Now just like any body shape, apples are just fine of a shape to have. But I knew that my waist was at least _some_ defined.

After having some doubts of the legitimacy of these quizzes, I researched celebrity body shapes. One thing I noticed as a trend, was that many women celebs who were said to be hourglasses, were actually _inverted triangles!_ [Can you spot them out?](https://blog.petitedressing.com/hourglass-celeb/) _(Hint: Look at Emma Roberts shoulder to hip ratio.)_

I realized that the quizzes were based on bust being larger than hips even though that’s not as humanly possible! _(Sofie Dossi is perhaps an exception)_ Instead a real test is based on shoulder VS hip ratio, which these quizzes get absolutely wrong.

When I did a male body shape quiz however, it turned out how I thought my measurements were: an inverted triangle.

> **Trapezoid Body Shape:**

> The trapezoid body shape, also known as the “wedge”, is characterized by broader shoulders and a narrower waist. Men with this body type often have a muscular upper body and a more tapered waistline.”

### Why the importance?

Most of the time to be true to ourselves and feel confident, we need to know the truth in how our body works so that we can better do what we need to support it, wouldn’t you think? Otherwise a person who keeps getting told they’re a rectangle might wear clothes that are unappealing to them, which I felt the case for myself. It wasn’t until I embraced more Korean fashion, which is tailored better to my shape, did I like how I dressed more.

With all the biases against certain body shapes, no wonder these quizzes seem to dislike inverted triangles —  even though as one myself, I prefer it more than the opposite, same as those who are vice versa.

### How do body types help identify body shapes?
Apple shoulder = round, roundness = endomorph
Inverted triangle = vice versa

There's a number of similarities between the two, yet it boils down to body type. Someone can be inbetween, like myself having primarily ectomorphic traits with some mesomorphic, and someone can be primarily mesomorphic with perhaps some endomorphic traits *(like Sofie Dosssi, a definite apple shape)*.
  

### Takeaway

Funny how I got the truth by going for a quiz that seemed not subjective to women stereotypes, or perhaps male stereotypes for that matter (though I suppose huge shoulders is what any guy wants).
